# Why Our Country Loaf Takes Four Days

Last January, we stopped using commercial yeast. Every loaf at Kessler's Bread now starts with a sourdough starter, a living culture of flour and water that ferments slowly and gives bread its flavor, its texture, and its crackling crust. The starter we use came from a crock my grandmother kept in 1962. It has been fed and kept alive through more decades than I can count, and it is the reason our bread tastes the way it does.

The change was harder than I expected. Commercial yeast gets a loaf ready in about four hours. Sourdough takes around twenty, from the first mix through the long, cool rise and the final bake. For four months, we lost money. Our ovens ran on a different schedule, our staff learned new timing, and I spent more nights than I'd like to admit wondering whether we had made a mistake.

When we finally counted what was left, we had kept three of our eleven breads. The rest didn't survive the transition, and that was a painful thing to let go of. But the three we kept are the ones people asked for most, and the country loaf became the center of everything we bake.

Something shifted by September. Sales of the country loaf went from about 60 a week to 140. Customers started telling us they came back for it, that it was the bread they reached for first. We didn't change our marketing. The bread changed, and people noticed.

One regular, Mr. Albright, told me recently that he has diabetes and that his blood sugar seems to handle our sourdough better than other bread he has eaten. I'm not a doctor, and I won't make health claims about our bread. If you have questions about your own diet, please talk with your doctor. What I can say is that he's a loyal customer, and I'm glad our bread is working for him.

If you haven't tried the country loaf, Saturday mornings are the best time to come in. We bake early, the shelves are full, and you can watch the morning rush while you wait. Pick up a loaf, ask us about the starter, and take one home. Then tell us what you think.

We'll be here on Main Street in Sidney, and the crock is still going.

— Ruth Kessler, Kessler's Bread
