**Hello, friends,**

The first hard frost came through this week, and the fields have changed with it. The tomato vines are gone and the kale is sweeter than it's been all year. Cold weather turns starches into sugars, so the carrots, leeks, and brassicas are at their best right now.

**What's in your box this week**

- **Winter squash:** Delicata and acorn. Delicata has thin, edible skin, so you can roast it whole, slices and all. Acorn keeps well on a cool counter for a few weeks.
- **Kale:** Lacinato (dinosaur) kale, which is tender enough to eat raw if you massage it with a little oil and salt for a minute.
- **Carrots:** Harvested after the frost, so they're extra sweet. Store them in the fridge in a bag with a few holes poked in it.
- **Leeks:** Slice them from the root end up and rinse well, since grit hides between the layers.
- **Sweet potatoes:** These cure in storage, so they'll taste sweeter in a few weeks than they do today.

**A simple way to use it all**

Roast a sheet pan of chopped carrots, leeks, and delicata squash at 425°F with olive oil, salt, and a few sprigs of thyme until the edges caramelize, about 25 minutes. Toss with the massaged kale and a squeeze of lemon. It's a full meal with almost no effort, and it keeps well for lunch.
