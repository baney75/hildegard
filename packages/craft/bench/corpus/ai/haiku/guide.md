# What to Bring for a Fall Steelhead Float Trip on the Pere Marquette

Fall steelhead on the Pere Marquette is a different kind of fishing. The water is cold, the leaves are turning, and the fish are moving upriver with purpose. Our float trips run from October through early December, and the right gear makes the difference between a great day on the water and a miserable one. Here's what to bring.

## Start With the Water Temperature

Expect water between 38 and 46 degrees. That's cold enough to make wet cotton dangerous and cold enough that you'll appreciate every layer you packed. Cold water pulls heat from your body much faster than cold air does, so plan your clothing around staying dry and warm, even on a sunny afternoon.

## Dress in Layers, Skip the Cotton

Cotton holds water and loses its insulation when wet, so leave the cotton tees and hoodies at home. Choose synthetic or wool materials instead:

- **Base layer:** Merino wool or synthetic long underwear, top and bottom.
- **Mid layer:** A fleece or lightweight wool sweater that insulates even when damp.
- **Outer layer:** A waterproof, windproof shell jacket and rain pants.
- **Head and hands:** A warm hat, a neck gaiter, and gloves or fingerless gloves you can still tie knots in.
- **Socks:** Wool or synthetic, and bring a spare pair in a dry bag.

Dressing in layers also lets you adjust as the day warms up or the wind picks up on the river. You can peel off a layer or add one without stopping the boat.

## Waders and Boots

Bring breathable waders. They keep you dry without trapping sweat, and they're far more comfortable for a full day of rowing, casting, and sitting in the boat.

For footwear, use wading boots with rubber or non-felt soles. Michigan has restricted felt soles on some rivers because felt can carry invasive species such as didymo (rock snot) from one river to another. Regulations vary by waterway, so the simplest rule is to leave felt at home entirely. I don't allow felt soles in my boat, and I'd rather you know that before you pack than find out at the launch.

Bring a pair of dry shoes or sandals for the drive and for the lunch break, too.

## Your Fishing License

A valid Michigan fishing license with an all-species endorsement is required. Buy it before you come. You're responsible for having it on you during the trip.

## What I Provide

You don't need to haul much gear. I supply:

- Rods and reels matched to steelhead fishing
- Flies and terminal tackle
- The drift boat, plus life jackets

Just bring your clothing, your waders and boots, your license, and whatever personal items make you comfortable, like sunglasses, a headlamp, snacks, water, and a small first-aid kit.

## What to Pack

- Breathable waders and non-felt wading boots
- Synthetic or wool base, mid, and outer layers, plus rain gear
- Warm hat, gloves, and neck gaiter
- Spare dry clothes in a waterproof bag
- Polarized sunglasses
- Fishing license with all-species endorsement
- Water, snacks, and a thermos of something hot
- Sunscreen (yes, even in fall, the sun reflects off the water)

## The Day Itself

We launch at 7 a.m. from the Gleason's Landing access, so plan to arrive a little early to get settled. Early morning is cold, and the river rewards those who are on the water when the light comes up. We'll float the river, read the runs, and work the fish as they hold in the pools and tailouts.

Trips are $525 for two people. Steelhead are strong, unpredictable, and a joy to catch on a cold fall morning. Come prepared, dress in wool and synthetics, and you'll have a day you won't forget.
