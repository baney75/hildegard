# Don't Let Your Mower Spend the Winter Gumming Up

Every spring, the same story walks through our door. Someone pulls the mower out of the shed, pulls the cord, and gets nothing but a cough or silence. About 80% of the spring repairs we see come down to one problem: a gummed-up carburetor caused by old gas.

## Why Old Gas Causes Trouble

Most gasoline sold today contains ethanol. Ethanol attracts moisture and breaks down faster than straight gasoline, and it can go bad in about 30 days. Once it does, it leaves a varnish-like residue that clogs the tiny jets and passages in the carburetor. A mower that sat all winter with fuel in the tank often needs a carburetor cleaning or rebuild before it runs right again.

A carb rebuild at our shop runs $85 to $120. A bottle of fuel stabilizer costs about $8. The math is easy.

## Your Two Options for Winter Storage

**Option 1: Run it dry.** Run the engine until it stops from lack of fuel. This empties the carburetor and fuel lines, so there's nothing left to go bad. It works well, but it takes some care to do it right.

**Option 2: Use fuel stabilizer.** Fill the tank with fresh gas, add stabilizer following the bottle's directions, then run the engine for about 5 minutes. That runs the treated fuel through the carburetor so the stabilizer protects the whole fuel system, not just the tank. Then store the mower with a full tank of treated fuel.

Either way, store the mower somewhere dry and avoid leaving it in a damp shed or a cold garage where condensation builds up.

## Safety First

Before you touch the blade for any reason, whether to clean it, check it, or sharpen it, pull the spark plug wire off the plug and tuck it away from the plug. A mower can start unexpectedly if the blade or engine gets bumped, and a spinning blade can cause serious injury. Take the two minutes to do this every time.

## Sharpen Your Blade Now

A dull blade tears grass instead of cutting it, which leaves brown tips and makes the lawn more vulnerable to disease. Winter is the best time to get the blade sharpened so it's ready when spring arrives.
