# The Shepherd Who Went Looking

Friends, turn with me to Luke chapter fifteen. Jesus has been eating with tax collectors and sinners, and the religious leaders are grumbling. So he tells them a story, and it is a story they would have understood in their bones.

*What man among you, having a hundred sheep, if he has lost one of them, does not leave the ninety-nine in the open country and go after the one that is lost until he finds it?*

Think about that question for a moment. A hundred sheep was a real flock, and a shepherd's livelihood depended on it. One sheep out of a hundred is a small loss by the numbers. Any sensible accountant would write it off. But Jesus does not describe a shepherd who shrugs. He describes a shepherd who leaves the ninety-nine and searches.

Anyone who has lived near sheep knows why. A sheep is not a clever animal. It wanders off after a patch of green grass, it gets distracted, and it cannot find its way home. It has no map and no sense of direction, and when it is lost it does not know it is lost. It simply goes on eating, further and further from the fold, until it is tangled in brambles or fallen into a ravine, bleating for help that may never come.

So the shepherd goes. He walks through the rocky hills, pushes through thorn bushes, calls out, and listens. When he finds the sheep, Jesus says, he lays it on his shoulders, rejoicing. He does not drag it home or scold it for wandering. He carries it. The sheep that could do nothing to save itself is carried all the way back.

Notice what Jesus is saying about God. The lost sheep is not punished. It is sought. The shepherd does not wait at home for the sheep to figure out its mistake. He comes looking. That is the heart of the Father's love, and it is the heart of the gospel. We were the ones who wandered. Some of us wandered far and knew it. Others of us wandered quietly, staying close enough to the flock to look respectable while our hearts drifted somewhere else. Either way, we could not find our own way back.

And then comes the part that should stop us. Jesus says there is more joy in heaven over one sinner who repents than over ninety-nine righteous persons who need no repentance. The religious leaders standing in front of him were the ninety-nine, and they did not like hearing that. But the point is not to shame the faithful. The point is that heaven's joy is not reserved for the perfectly kept. Heaven rejoices when the lost one comes home.

Some of you have been the lost sheep. Maybe you are there now. You have drifted from prayer, from worship, from the people who love you, and you have told yourself that you will come back when things are in order. But the shepherd does not wait for you to get your life in order. He is already on the hillside, calling your name. The only question is whether you will let him carry you.

And some of you are the shepherds, or you are being asked to become them. Who in your life has wandered? Who has stopped coming? Who has been quietly slipping away while the rest of us kept busy with the ninety-nine? Jesus does not say the shepherd sends a letter or posts a notice. He goes out. He walks the rough ground. He takes the trouble, because one person matters to him, and so this church must be a community that goes looking for the ones who have gone missing.

So let us pray this week for the lost sheep we know by name. Let us ask the Good Shepherd to give us eyes to see who is missing from the fold, and courage to walk out and call them home. And let us rest in the promise that the Shepherd has already come looking for each of us.

Amen.
