# Sitting on the Porch

Luke 15 begins with tax collectors and sinners pressing in close to hear Jesus, and the Pharisees and scribes grumbling, "This man welcomes sinners and eats with them." Jesus answers them with three stories. Today I want to stay with the first two, the shepherd and the woman with the coin, because I think Jesus chose two very ordinary searches on purpose.

A shepherd has a hundred sheep and loses one. Most of us would do the arithmetic and say ninety-nine is a good number. But he leaves the flock in the open country and goes looking. A woman has ten silver coins and loses one. So she lights a lamp, sweeps the whole house, and searches carefully until she finds it. Neither of them is in a hurry to make a point. They simply do not stop looking.

Last month a young man from our youth group walked away from this church. His father had died, and the funeral was the last time many of us saw him in a pew. He stopped answering texts. He stopped showing up. Some of you felt that absence as keenly as I did.

A few of us talked about what to do. We didn't have a plan, and I'm not sure we had the right words. So on a Tuesday night, three of us drove to his apartment. We didn't call first. We climbed the stairs, and when he opened the door, we asked if we could sit on his porch for a while. He didn't tell us to leave, so we stayed. We sat there for about an hour. He didn't say a word. Neither did we, most of the time. We weren't fixing anything. We were just not leaving.

I used to think the point of a story like this was the moment the lost thing comes home and everyone cheers. I don't think that anymore. The shepherd does carry the sheep home, but first he has to go out into the dark where it wandered. The woman has to get down with a lamp and search the corners. The searching is the costly part, and Jesus holds it up as the heart of God.

Two weeks later, he came to youth group. That's all I'll say about it. I'm not going to tell you everything is resolved. Grief doesn't keep a schedule, and a young man who lost his father is still carrying something heavy. But he came back, and I believe part of the reason is that someone kept coming back for him.

Jesus ends both parables the same way: there is joy in heaven over one sinner who repents. I want to be careful here. Heaven's joy is not a reward for good attendance. It is the response of a God who goes looking for people who have wandered, and who does not give up on them when they go quiet.

So here is my question for you this week. Who has gone quiet on you? Maybe it's a child, an old friend, a brother who stopped returning calls, or a neighbor who used to sit near you on Sunday. You may not have a clever thing to say. You may not need one. Sometimes the faithful thing is to drive over, climb the stairs, and sit on the porch for an hour.

The Good Shepherd did not wait for the lost sheep to find its own way home. He went out after it. May we learn to do the same.
