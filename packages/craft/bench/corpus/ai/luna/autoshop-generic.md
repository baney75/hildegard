# Winter Battery Care: How to Avoid a Cold-Weather No-Start

Cold mornings put extra strain on your car battery. Low temperatures slow the chemical reactions that produce electrical power, while your engine needs more power to turn over. A battery that seemed fine in mild weather may struggle when the temperature drops.

A little preparation can help you avoid an inconvenient no-start.

## Watch for signs of a weakening battery

Pay attention if the engine cranks more slowly than usual, the headlights dim while starting, or you need a jump-start. These can be signs that the battery is losing its ability to hold a charge. Corrosion around the terminals or a battery that’s several years old also deserves attention.

A warning light may not appear before a battery fails. If you’re unsure about its condition, have it tested before the coldest weather arrives.

## Keep the battery and connections in good shape

Dirt and corrosion can interfere with the connection between the battery and your vehicle. If you notice a powdery buildup around the terminals, have it cleaned safely and check that the connections are secure. Avoid touching both terminals with a tool or other metal object.

Make sure the battery is firmly held in place, too. Excessive movement can damage it over time.

## Take short trips into account

Starting the engine uses a burst of battery power. The alternator needs time to replace it, so frequent short trips may leave the battery undercharged—especially in winter, when heaters, defrosters, lights, and heated seats add electrical demand.

If your driving is mostly short hops, a battery and charging-system check can help catch a problem before it leaves you stranded.

## Be ready for a cold-weather emergency

Keep jumper cables or a portable jump starter in your vehicle, and learn how to use them safely for your car. Check your owner’s manual for the correct jump-start procedure; connecting cables incorrectly can damage vehicle electronics or cause injury.

If your car won’t start, repeated attempts can drain the battery further. A professional diagnosis can determine whether the issue is the battery, charging system, starter, or another part of the vehicle.
