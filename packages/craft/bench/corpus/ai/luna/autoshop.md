# Is Your Car Battery Ready for a Wyoming Winter?

*By Luis Moreno, Moreno Auto*

Last January, I jump-started 42 cars in the first week alone. A lot of those mornings could have started differently with a quick battery check before the cold set in.

Cold weather makes a battery work harder just when it has less power to give. At 32°F, a battery loses about a third of its starting power. At zero, it loses about half. That can be enough to turn a slow crank into a car that won’t start.

Most car batteries last three to five years. Here in Wyoming, ours average about four. If yours is getting close to that age, or you’ve noticed the engine cranking slowly or the dash lights dimming while the car idles, get it tested before the first cold snap.

At Moreno Auto, a battery load test is free and takes about ten minutes. If you need a replacement, installation typically runs $140 to $220, depending on the vehicle and battery.

You can also take a look at the battery terminals. If you see corrosion, clean it off with baking soda and a wire brush. A clean connection helps the battery deliver power reliably. If you’re not comfortable doing that yourself, we can check the terminals when you come in.

A little preparation now can save you a cold morning waiting for a jump-start. Stop by Moreno Auto in Cheyenne for a free battery load test before winter arrives.
