# Finishing a Walnut Dining Table

Walnut has a way of making a dining table feel warm and inviting before the first coat of finish goes on. Its color ranges from soft honey brown to deep chocolate, and its grain can be straight and quiet or full of movement. The right finish brings out that character while helping the table stand up to daily meals, spills, and years of use.

## Start with careful sanding

A good finish begins with a smooth, evenly prepared surface. We sand progressively through finer grits, removing marks from each previous pass before moving on. Extra care goes to the tabletop, where raking light can reveal scratches that are easy to miss.

Before finishing, we remove every trace of dust. Left behind, fine sanding dust can settle into the finish and dull the surface.

## Choose a finish for the way the table will be used

Dining tables need a finish that looks good and handles everyday life. A penetrating oil can enrich walnut’s color and leave a natural, hand-rubbed feel. A film finish, such as varnish, creates a more protective surface that resists moisture and wear.

There’s no single right choice for every table. The best finish depends on the look you want, how the table will be used, and how much upkeep you’re comfortable with. We apply samples to scrap walnut first, since the same finish can look different on different boards.

## Apply thin, even coats

The key to a clean finish is patience. We apply each coat evenly, follow the product’s directions, and allow it to dry fully before recoating. If the finish calls for sanding between coats, a light pass helps smooth the surface and gives the next coat a good base.

It’s tempting to rush this stage, especially when the table is nearly done. But giving each coat the time it needs makes a noticeable difference in the final feel and appearance.

## Give the finish time to cure

A surface may feel dry before it’s ready for regular use. Curing takes longer than drying, so we follow the finish maker’s guidance before placing objects or serving meals on the table. During that time, gentle handling helps protect the new surface.

## Keep walnut looking its best

Once the table is in use, simple care goes a long way. Wipe up spills promptly, use coasters and trivets, and clean with a soft cloth and a product suited to the finish. Avoid harsh cleaners and standing water, which can damage the protective layer.

Walnut changes gradually as it meets light and time. A thoughtful finish protects the table while letting that natural character remain visible—a surface made to gather around, day after day.
