# A Walnut Dining Table That Gets Better With Dinner

I make about 30 dining tables a year at Oberg Woodworks in Duluth, Georgia. For each walnut table, I use a hand-rubbed oil and wax finish. It brings out the grain, feels natural to the touch, and makes everyday wear easier to care for.

The finish takes time. I apply three coats of Danish oil, allowing 24 hours between coats, then finish with paste wax. Walnut will darken as it’s exposed to sunlight, especially during its first year, developing a richer color over time.

Some finishes form a hard film over the wood. Polyurethane, for example, can look plastic-like and may chip. An oil finish works differently: it sinks into the wood, so the table can be refreshed as it ages. If water rings appear, they can be sanded out with 400-grit sandpaper and the area re-oiled in an afternoon.

I made a walnut table for the Hollis family in 2009. They’ve shared weekly dinners around it for 15 years, and it has needed just one re-oil. That’s the kind of life I hope for every table: years of meals, conversation, and use, with straightforward care along the way.

The oil finish adds about $200 to the price of a table. In return, you get a surface that shows walnut’s character and can be renewed when life leaves a mark.
