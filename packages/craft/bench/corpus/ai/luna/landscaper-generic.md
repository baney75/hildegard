# Fall Lawn Care: Set Your Grass Up for a Healthy Spring

Fall is one of the best times to care for your lawn. Cooler temperatures and regular rainfall help grass recover from summer stress and build strong roots before winter. A few timely tasks now can make a noticeable difference when spring arrives.

## Keep mowing while the grass grows

Don’t stop mowing just because the weather has cooled. As long as the grass is growing, keep cutting it and gradually lower the mower to the height recommended for your grass type. Avoid cutting it too short: scalping can weaken the lawn and leave it more vulnerable to winter damage.

A good rule is to remove no more than one-third of the grass blade at a time. Keep mower blades sharp for a clean cut, and mulch light layers of fallen leaves into the lawn if they aren’t covering the grass.

## Clear heavy leaves

A thick layer of leaves can block sunlight and trap moisture, creating conditions that stress or damage grass. Rake or mulch leaves regularly, especially after windy days. If you mulch them, make sure the pieces are small enough to settle between the grass blades rather than form a mat.

## Seed thin or bare areas

Fall’s milder weather gives many cool-season grasses time to sprout and establish roots before winter. If your lawn has thin patches, loosen the soil, spread seed suited to your region and lawn conditions, and keep the area consistently moist while the seed germinates.

For larger areas, overseeding can help create a denser lawn. Aerating before seeding may improve seed-to-soil contact, particularly where the ground is compacted.

## Aerate compacted soil

Foot traffic, play, and equipment can compact soil over time, making it harder for water, air, and nutrients to reach the roots. Core aeration removes small plugs of soil and can help relieve compaction. Fall is a useful time to aerate many cool-season lawns, especially before overseeding.

## Water when needed

Cooler weather usually reduces the need for watering, but new seed and recently planted turf still need consistent moisture. Established grass may also need water during a dry stretch. Water deeply when necessary, following local watering guidance, and avoid leaving the lawn soggy.

## Apply fertilizer thoughtfully

Fall feeding can support root growth and help cool-season grass recover from summer. Choose a fertilizer appropriate for your grass and soil, and follow the label for timing and application rates. Local conditions vary, so a soil test can help determine whether fertilizer is needed and which nutrients to apply.

## Tidy the lawn before winter

Pick up branches, toys, and other objects before the first hard freeze. Items left on the grass can flatten or smother it. Keep leaves from piling up, and make a final mow while the lawn is still actively growing.

A little attention in fall helps your lawn enter winter in better shape and gives it a stronger start in spring. If you’re unsure which steps are right for your grass or soil, a local lawn care professional can help you plan the season.
