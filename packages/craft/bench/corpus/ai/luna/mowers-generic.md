# How to Put Your Lawn Mower Away for Winter

A little care before winter storage can help your mower start more easily in spring and reduce fuel and moisture problems while it sits. Set aside about half an hour, and let the engine cool before you begin.

## 1. Clean the mower

Brush or scrape away grass, leaves, and dirt from the deck and around the engine. Grass packed under the deck holds moisture and can encourage rust. If you use water, keep it away from the air filter, muffler, and electrical parts, then dry the mower thoroughly.

For a riding mower, clear debris from the footrests and around the engine as well.

## 2. Handle the fuel

Old gasoline can break down during storage and cause hard starting or a clogged carburetor. Check your owner’s manual for the right approach for your model:

- **Drain the fuel system:** Use a hand pump to remove fuel from the tank, then run the engine outdoors until it stops. Never siphon by mouth, and keep fuel away from flames, sparks, and hot surfaces.
- **Use fresh fuel with stabilizer:** Add the amount of stabilizer listed on its label, then run the mower for a few minutes so treated fuel reaches the carburetor. This works only if the product and your mower’s manual allow it.

Don’t leave untreated gasoline sitting in the mower all winter. Store any fuel you keep in an approved, sealed container in a cool, well-ventilated place, away from living areas and ignition sources.

## 3. Change the oil and service the air filter

If the oil is due for a change, do it before storage. Warm oil drains more easily, but shut off the engine and let it cool enough to handle safely. Refill with the oil grade and amount specified in your manual.

Inspect the air filter and clean or replace it as directed. A dirty or damaged filter can make spring startup harder.

## 4. Check the blade and spark plug

Look for a bent, cracked, or badly worn blade. If it needs sharpening or replacement, arrange that before the mower is needed again. Wear gloves when handling the blade, and disconnect the spark plug wire first so the engine cannot start accidentally.

Check the spark plug and replace it if the manual’s maintenance schedule calls for one. Reconnect the plug wire when you’re finished.

## 5. Prepare the battery, if equipped

For a riding mower or battery-powered start system, follow the manual’s instructions for battery storage. Some batteries should be removed and stored indoors; others can stay installed with a compatible maintainer. Keep batteries dry and away from freezing conditions unless the manufacturer says otherwise. Disconnect the negative cable first when removing a battery.

## 6. Store it in a dry, safe place

Park the mower on a level surface in a dry, ventilated area, away from furnaces, water heaters, and other ignition sources. Don’t store gasoline-powered equipment where fuel vapors could collect in a living space. If the mower has a fuel shutoff valve, set it as the manual directs.

For a walk-behind mower, store it in its normal position unless the manual specifically permits another orientation. Tilting it the wrong way can let oil or fuel enter parts where they don’t belong.

## 7. Make a spring note

Write down any parts or maintenance the mower will need before its first cut—such as a blade, spark plug, filter, or battery check. That way, you can take care of it before the grass gets tall.

If you’re unsure whether to drain the fuel, how to position your mower, or what service it needs, check the owner’s manual or bring it to our shop. A quick inspection now can save you a frustrating first start in spring.
