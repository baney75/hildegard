# Put Your Mower Away Right: A Little Fall Care Can Save a Spring Repair

When mowing season winds down, it’s tempting to park the mower in the shed and forget about it. But old gas can turn that easy spring start into a carburetor repair.

About 80% of the spring repairs I see at Dale’s Small Engine in Marion are caused by carburetors gummed up with old gas. Ethanol gas can go bad in about 30 days, so it may not make it through an Indiana winter in good shape.

Before storing your mower, either run the fuel system dry or add fuel stabilizer to the gas and run the mower for five minutes. That gives the treated fuel a chance to reach the carburetor. A bottle of stabilizer costs about $8. A carburetor rebuild at my shop typically runs $85 to $120.

While you’re getting the mower ready for storage, check the blade. **Pull the spark plug wire before touching the blade** to help prevent an accidental start. If it needs sharpening, I’ll sharpen it for $12 when you drop it off by November 15.

A few minutes of fall maintenance can make a big difference when it’s time to mow again. Stop by Dale’s Small Engine in Marion and get your mower ready for winter.
