A Shepherd Who Searches

Jesus tells of a shepherd who has one hundred sheep. When one wanders away, he leaves the ninety-nine and goes after the lost one “until he finds it.” Then he lays it across his shoulders and comes home rejoicing, calling his friends and neighbors to celebrate with him.

It is a simple story, but its comfort runs deep. The shepherd does not wait for the sheep to find its own way back. He searches. He keeps going. And when he finds the sheep, he does not scold it or make it walk home alone. He carries it.

Jesus told this parable to people who knew what it felt like to be judged as beyond reach. Some had made mistakes. Some had been pushed to the margins. Some simply carried the quiet ache of believing they did not belong. The religious leaders around Jesus grumbled that he welcomed such people and ate with them. So Jesus answered with a picture of God’s heart: God notices the one who is missing, and God’s joy is in bringing the lost home.

We can hear ourselves in the sheep that has wandered. Sometimes we lose our way through choices we regret. Sometimes grief, exhaustion, or disappointment makes familiar paths hard to follow. Sometimes the distance grows so gradually that we do not realize how far we have gone until we feel alone. Whatever the reason, the parable does not say that we must first make ourselves worthy of rescue. It says that we are sought.

That does not mean our choices do not matter, or that the pain they cause is insignificant. Love tells the truth. But the shepherd’s first movement is toward the sheep. God’s mercy meets us where we are and makes a way toward home. We may still have steps to take, apologies to offer, or help to receive. We do not take those steps alone.

The parable also asks something of the church. If God rejoices over the one who returns, then our welcome should make room for people who are finding their way back. We are not asked to decide who deserves a place at the table. We are asked to share the shepherd’s joy: to notice who is missing, to reach out with patience, and to receive one another with compassion.

That kind of welcome can be practical and quiet. It may mean listening without rushing to judgment, checking in on someone who has been absent, or offering help without demanding a full explanation. It may mean making space for questions and for the slow work of healing. We cannot carry every burden for one another, but we can help make the way home less lonely.

And sometimes we are the ninety-nine, safe together while someone else is still out in the dark. The story reminds us that God’s care is not diminished by being shared. The shepherd’s search does not make the flock less loved. Every sheep matters. Every person matters. The joy of God grows as the lost are found and brought home.

Jesus ends by describing the joy in heaven over one person who turns toward God. That joy is not reserved for the perfect, the certain, or the people who never wandered. It is the joy of a homecoming. It is the joy of being found, lifted up, and welcomed again.

So if you feel far away, hear this good news: you are not forgotten. The shepherd is searching, and the road home begins with being found. And if you are walking with someone who feels lost, let your patience and kindness reflect the shepherd’s heart. There is room for every one of us in God’s care, and there is joy whenever someone comes home.
