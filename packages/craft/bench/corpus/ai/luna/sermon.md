# When the Lost Are Found

**Luke 15:1–10**

Jesus tells of a shepherd who leaves ninety-nine sheep in the open country and goes looking for the one that is lost. When he finds it, he joyfully carries it home. Then he tells of a woman who lights a lamp and sweeps her house until she finds a missing coin. Both stories end with a celebration. And Jesus tells us why: “There is joy before the angels of God over one sinner who repents.”

These parables are not a set of steps for getting someone back. They are a glimpse of God’s heart. He sees the one who is missing. He seeks. He rejoices when the lost are found.

Last month, after his father’s funeral, a boy from our youth group walked away from the church. Three of us drove to his apartment on a Tuesday night and sat on his porch for an hour. He never said a word.

There was no breakthrough, no moving speech, no sign that we had found the right thing to say. We sat with him. Then we left, still not knowing what he was thinking or what would happen next.

Two weeks later, he came to youth group.

I don’t share that as a formula. We cannot promise that if we show up, wait long enough, or say the right words, someone will return on our schedule. Grief does not follow our schedule. Neither does faith. The boy’s return was not something we could produce. It was a gift—and a reminder that silence does not mean someone is beyond reach.

In Luke 15, Jesus is answering people who complain that he welcomes sinners and eats with them. His stories turn their complaint into a window: this is what God is like. God does not regard the lost as an inconvenience or a statistic. He searches for them, and their return brings joy in heaven.

Sometimes seeking looks like a long journey after a sheep. Sometimes it looks like a lamp lit in a dark house, a patient search through familiar corners. And sometimes, as far as we can tell, it looks like sitting on a porch without getting an answer.

The parables do not ask us to pretend we can rescue anyone by ourselves. They invite us to reflect the care of the Shepherd: to notice who is missing, to make room for sorrow, to keep the door open, and to trust God with what we cannot see. We may not know what another person is carrying, or when they will be ready to speak. We can still let them know they are not forgotten.

Jesus’ words also offer hope to anyone who feels lost. You do not have to find your way back alone. The Shepherd seeks you. God’s welcome is not reserved for people who have everything sorted out or can explain their absence. His joy is in bringing the lost home.

And when someone returns, let the church be a place of welcome, not interrogation; a place where a person can come in quietly and be received with love. Heaven rejoices over one who is found. May we learn to share that joy, with patience for the road home and gratitude for every step.

**Pastor Dale Henning**  
Faith Bible Church · Lima, Ohio
