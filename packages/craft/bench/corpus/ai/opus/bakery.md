# Twenty Hours for a Loaf of Bread

In January 2025, I did something that a lot of people told me was a bad idea. I stopped using commercial yeast at Kessler's Bread and switched everything over to sourdough.

The starter came from my grandmother's crock. She began keeping it in 1962, and it has been fed and cared for in my family ever since. For years it lived in my kitchen at home, and I baked with it for myself on weekends. At some point I started asking myself why the bread I made for my own table was different from the bread I sold on Main Street. I didn't have a good answer, so I brought the crock to the bakery.

## The hard part

I want to be honest about what that change cost us.

A loaf made with commercial yeast takes about four hours from mixing to cooling rack. A sourdough loaf, done the way my grandmother did it, takes about twenty. That isn't a small adjustment. It changes when you start your day, how much you can bake, and how many loaves are ready when the door opens.

For four months, we lost money. There were mornings the shelves weren't as full as you were used to seeing them. Some of you noticed, and some of you were kind enough not to mention it.

We also had to make some tough choices. We used to bake eleven different breads. Not all of them worked as sourdough, and not all of them were worth the twenty hours. We kept three. Letting the others go wasn't easy, and I know a few of you still miss your favorites. I miss some of them too.

## What came back

Then something started to turn.

The bread that came out of those long, slow mornings was better. The crust crackled. The inside was open and chewy and tasted like something, not just like bread. And people kept coming back for it, especially the country loaf.

Last year we were selling about 60 country loaves a week. By September, we were selling 140. I didn't run a single ad. That happened because you tried it, liked it, and told your neighbors.

## A note from a regular

One of our regulars, Mr. Albright, has diabetes. A while back he told me that, in his own experience, his blood sugar seems to handle our sourdough better than other breads he's eaten. He was glad to have bread back in his life, and I was touched that he shared that with me.

I'm a baker, not a doctor, and I'm not going to tell you this bread will do anything for your health. Everyone's body is different. If you're managing a condition like diabetes, please talk with your doctor about what's right for you. I'm just passing along what one customer told me, because it meant a lot to hear.

## Come try the country loaf

If you haven't had it yet, or if it's been a while since you stopped in, come see us on a Saturday morning. The country loaf is the best thing we make, and Saturday is the best day to get it fresh.

It's the same starter my grandmother kept in 1962, the same slow process, and twenty hours of patience in every loaf. I think you'll taste the difference.

We're on Main Street in Sidney. I'll see you Saturday.

— Ruth Kessler, Kessler's Bread
