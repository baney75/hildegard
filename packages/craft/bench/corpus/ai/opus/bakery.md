# Twenty Hours: Why We Changed Our Bread

In January 2025, I did something at Kessler's Bread that I'd been thinking about for years. I stopped using commercial yeast and started baking with a sourdough starter.

It wasn't just any starter. It came from my grandmother's crock, the same stoneware crock she kept on her kitchen counter starting in 1962. I grew up watching her feed it, cover it with a dish towel, and fuss over it like a member of the family. When I finally decided to build our bread around it, it felt less like a business decision and more like coming home.

## The Hard Part

I'll be honest with you: the first few months were rough.

A loaf made with commercial yeast takes about four hours from mixing to the oven. A loaf made with a natural starter takes around twenty. That's not a typo. The dough needs time to ferment slowly, develop flavor, and build the structure that gives sourdough its chewy crumb and crackling crust. You can't hurry it, and believe me, I tried.

That extra time changed everything about how we run the bakery. We had to plan a full day ahead and rework every schedule. We lost money for four straight months. There were weeks I sat at the kitchen table with the books open and wondered whether I'd made a terrible mistake.

## Fewer Breads, Better Bread

Something had to give, so we made a tough call. We used to bake eleven different breads. Today we bake three.

Saying goodbye to some of those recipes was hard, and I know a few of you still miss your favorites. But narrowing down meant we could give each loaf the time and attention it deserves. Every bread that leaves our ovens now is one I'm truly proud of.

## The Country Loaf

Of the three we kept, the one that's really taken off is our country loaf. It has a deep golden crust, a soft and open crumb, and that gentle tang that only comes from a long, slow fermentation. It's wonderful toasted with butter, alongside a bowl of soup, or torn straight from the loaf on the drive home. No judgment here.

You noticed, too. In January we were selling about 60 country loaves a week. By September, that number had climbed to 140. Every one of those loaves is a little thank-you from me to the folks in Sidney who stuck with us through the lean months.

## A Note From a Regular

One of our longtime customers, Mr. Albright, stopped by the counter a while back to tell me something that stayed with me. He lives with diabetes, and he said that in his own experience, our sourdough sits better with him than the bread he used to buy.

I want to be careful here. I'm a baker, not a doctor, and I'm not making any health claims about our bread. Everyone's body is different, and if you have questions about your diet, your doctor is the right person to ask. Still, hearing a regular say he can enjoy our bread again meant the world to me.
