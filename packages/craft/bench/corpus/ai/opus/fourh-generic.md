# Clover Notes: November 2026

## A Season of Gratitude

The leaves are down, the fair ribbons are hanging on bedroom walls, and the days are getting shorter. November is a natural time to pause and give thanks for the members who showed up, the parents who drove, the volunteers who taught, and the community that cheered us on.

To every member who tried something new this year, whether that meant showing an animal for the first time, giving a demonstration, or leading the pledge at a meeting: thank you. You are what makes this club grow.

## Looking Back on 4-H Week

Last month we celebrated National 4-H Week, and our members did us proud. Thank you to everyone who wore green, put up posters, and told friends and classmates what 4-H is all about. Several new families have asked about joining because of your efforts. Please give them a warm welcome when they visit.

## Enrollment Is Open

It's time to re-enroll for the new 4-H year. Returning members, please complete your enrollment and select your projects as soon as you can. Early enrollment helps our leaders plan workshops, order supplies, and line up project volunteers.

New members are always welcome. If you know a young person who loves animals, building, cooking, science, art, or just trying new things, bring them to our next meeting.

## Record Books

If your record book from last year isn't turned in yet, now is the time to finish it. Record books aren't busywork. They're your story of what you learned, how you grew, and what you accomplished. They also count toward awards and recognition, so don't leave that work on the table.

**Tips for finishing strong:**
- Fill in your project goals and whether you met them
- Include photos, newspaper clippings, and ribbons
- List your community service hours, even the small ones
- Ask a parent or leader to look it over before you turn it in

## Giving Back This Holiday Season

As we head into Thanksgiving, our club will be organizing a community service project to support local families. We'll collect nonperishable food items at our next meeting. Canned vegetables, soup, peanut butter, pasta, rice, and boxed stuffing are especially appreciated. Every can counts, and every member who contributes earns service hours.

We're also brainstorming ideas for a December project. Card-making for nursing home residents, a mitten and hat drive, and caroling have all been suggested. Bring your ideas to the meeting and we'll vote.

## Officer Corner

Our newly elected officers are settling into their roles. If you have an idea for a meeting program, a guest speaker, a field trip, or a fun activity, talk to any officer. This is your club, and your voice shapes what we do.

**A reminder to all members:** practice parliamentary procedure at meetings. Making a motion, seconding it, and voting are skills you'll use for the rest of your life.

## Project Spotlight: Winter Prep

For animal project members, colder weather means extra care:
- Check water sources daily for ice
- Provide extra bedding and shelter from wind
- Adjust feed as needed. Animals burn more calories staying warm
- Keep up with your record-keeping through the winter months

For everyone else, winter is the perfect time to dig into indoor projects like foods and nutrition, sewing, photography, woodworking, robotics, and public speaking. Start planning your demonstration or illustrated talk now so you're ready when contest season arrives.

## Family Fun: Thanksgiving Gratitude Challenge

Try this at home: each night this month, every person at the dinner table names one thing they're grateful for. Write them on paper leaves and tape them to a window or wall. By Thanksgiving, you'll have a whole tree of thankfulness.
