# What to Bring on a Fall Steelhead Float Trip

Fall is the season we wait for all year. The leaves turn, the crowds thin out, and fresh steelhead push up the river with the first good rains. A day in the drift boat this time of year can be the best fishing you'll have all season, but it can also be cold, wet, and long if you show up unprepared.

We supply the boat, rods, reels, terminal tackle, and bait. Here's what you should bring to stay warm, dry, and focused on the fish.

## Dress in Layers

Fall mornings on the river start cold, often near freezing, and can warm up quickly by early afternoon. Layering lets you adjust as the day goes on.

- **Base layer:** Synthetic or merino wool long underwear, top and bottom. Skip cotton. It holds moisture and pulls heat from your body once it gets damp.
- **Mid layer:** A fleece jacket, wool sweater, or light puffy. Bring two if you run cold.
- **Outer layer:** A good waterproof rain jacket and rain pants. This is the most important thing on the list. Even on a dry day, spray, drizzle, and wet lines will find you. A jacket with a hood is ideal.

## Keep Your Hands, Head, and Feet Warm

Most people get cold in the extremities first, and cold hands make it hard to fish.

- **Warm hat:** A wool or fleece beanie, plus a ball cap for rain and glare.
- **Gloves:** Fingerless wool gloves or fishing-specific gloves let you work a reel and still feel your line. A pair of warmer gloves for the ride between spots is a nice addition.
- **Footwear:** Waterproof boots with good traction. Knee-high rubber boots work well. If you own waders, bring them, as we sometimes get out and fish from shore.
- **Wool socks:** Wear a thick pair and pack a spare in a dry bag.
- **Hand warmers:** Cheap, small, and worth their weight on a frosty morning.

## Gear and Essentials

- **Polarized sunglasses:** They cut glare, protect your eyes from flying hooks, and help you see fish and structure in the water. Amber or copper lenses work best in fall's low light.
- **Fishing license and steelhead tag:** Purchase these before the trip and keep them on you. We can't fish you without them.
- **Small dry bag or backpack:** For your phone, wallet, keys, spare layers, and anything else that needs to stay dry.
- **Sunscreen and lip balm:** Even on overcast days, the water reflects UV.
- **Any medications you need:** Including motion sickness pills if you're prone to it, though river drifts are usually smooth.

## Food and Drink

Fall trips are full-day affairs, and staying fueled helps you stay warm.

- **Lunch and snacks:** Pack something easy to eat with one hand. Sandwiches, jerky, trail mix, and granola bars all work well.
- **Water:** At least a liter or two. Cold weather can hide how much you're sweating and dehydrating.
- **Something warm:** A thermos of coffee, tea, or soup goes a long way on a chilly morning.

## Nice to Have

- **Camera or phone in a waterproof case:** You'll want a photo when that chrome hen comes to the net.
- **Small towel:** For drying hands after releasing fish.
- **Extra change of clothes in the car:** You'll appreciate it on the drive home.
- **Tip for your guide:** Customary and always appreciated if you enjoyed your day.

## What to Leave at Home

- Cotton clothing, including jeans and hoodies
- Large hard-sided coolers (space in the boat is limited)
- Your own rods, unless you've talked with us first. We're happy to fish your gear, but let us know ahead of time.

## A Few Final Tips

Check the weather the night before, then plan for it to be a little colder and wetter than the forecast says. It's always easier to take off a layer than to wish you'd brought one. Arrive on time, well rested, and ready to fish. Fall steelhead often bite best in that first hour of light.

Most importantly, come with patience. Steelhead are called the fish of a thousand casts for a reason. When it finally happens, the reward is worth every one of them.
