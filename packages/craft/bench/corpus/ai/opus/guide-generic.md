# What to Bring on Your Fall Steelhead Float Trip

There's nothing quite like a fall steelhead float. The cottonwoods are turning gold, the morning fog hangs low over the water, and every swing of the fly or drift of the bobber carries the chance of a chrome-bright fish that can peel line faster than you thought possible.

Fall weather on the river is unpredictable, though. A trip can start in frost, warm to sunshine by noon, and end in a cold drizzle. Packing right is the difference between a day you'll talk about for years and one you spend counting the minutes until the take-out. Here's what we recommend bringing.

## Dress in Layers

The single most important thing you can do is layer. You'll be sitting in a drift boat for much of the day, and a seated angler gets cold faster than one who's hiking.

**Base layer:** Synthetic or merino wool long underwear, top and bottom. Skip the cotton. Once it's damp, it pulls heat from your body all day.

**Mid layer:** A fleece jacket or light puffy. Bring a second mid layer if the forecast looks cold; you can always stuff it in a dry bag.

**Outer layer:** A quality waterproof, breathable rain jacket with a hood. Even if the sky looks clear at the launch, bring it. Rain pants are a smart addition if you won't be wearing waders.

**Warm socks:** Wool, and pack a spare pair in a zip-top bag. Dry socks at lunch feel like a luxury.

## Keep Your Extremities Happy

Cold hands make it hard to tie knots, feel strikes, and land fish.

- **Fingerless gloves or fishing gloves** that let you manage line
- **A warm hat or beanie** for mornings, plus a **ball cap** for glare once the sun comes up
- **A neck gaiter or buff** to block wind
- **Hand warmers** tucked in your pockets are cheap insurance

## Waders and Boots

We often get out of the boat to fish good runs, so waders are a big help. Breathable waders with a layer of fleece underneath work well in the fall. Wear wading boots with good traction; river rocks are slick, especially when they're covered in fall algae. If you have studded or felt soles and your state allows them, bring them.

If you don't own waders, let us know when you book and we'll talk through options.

## Polarized Sunglasses

These aren't optional. Polarized glasses cut glare so you can read the water, spot fish, and see your line. They also protect your eyes from errant hooks and weights on windy days. Amber or copper lenses are great in the low, flat light common in the fall.

## Licenses and Paperwork

- **A valid fishing license** for the state you'll be fishing
- **A steelhead tag or punch card**, if required
- **Photo ID**

Please buy your license before the trip. Many put-ins have no cell service, so it's hard to buy one at the river.

## Food and Drink

We take care of lunch and drinks on our full-day trips, but feel free to bring:

- Your favorite **snacks**, like jerky, trail mix, or granola bars
- A **thermos of coffee or hot cocoa** for those frosty early hours
- **Any specific drinks** you prefer

Let us know about food allergies or dietary needs ahead of time so we can plan accordingly.

## Personal Items

- **Sunscreen and lip balm.** The fall sun reflecting off the water can still burn.
- **Any medications** you need during the day
- **A small dry bag** for your phone, keys, wallet, and extra layers
- **A phone or camera** for photos. Trust us, you'll want to remember that fish.
- **Cash for gratuity**, if you feel your guide earned it

## Gear: What We Provide

We supply quality rods, reels, line, flies, and terminal tackle on every trip, matched to current conditions and the techniques that are working. If you have a favorite rod or setup, you're welcome to bring it along, and we're happy to help get it rigged.

## What to Leave at Home

- **Cotton clothing**, especially jeans and hoodies
- **Heavy luggage or hard-sided coolers.** Boat space is limited.
- **High expectations about the forecast.** Some of the best steelhead days come in the worst weather.

## The Most Important Thing to Bring

Patience and a good attitude. Steelhead are called "the fish of a thousand casts" for a reason. Some days they come easy, some days you earn every one. Either way, you'll be spending the day on a beautiful river during one of the best seasons of the year.
