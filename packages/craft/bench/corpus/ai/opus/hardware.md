# Our Story

In 1955, my grandfather Walt Becker borrowed $2,000 and opened a hardware store in Hastings. That was a lot of money to bet on a building full of nails and paint cans, but Walt figured a town that builds things needs a place to buy the parts. Seventy years later, we're still here, and the bet still looks like a good one.

## What We Do

We sell the things that keep a house standing and a weekend project moving: paint, plumbing supplies, and the fasteners you actually need. Our fasteners are sold by the pound, so if you need three bolts, you buy three bolts, not a blister pack of twenty. We cut keys while you wait, and we cut glass to size for windows, picture frames, and tabletops.

In 1982, my dad added the garden center. Today it's where a lot of Hastings gets its spring started, with seeds and soil, tomato cages, and somebody around to tell you why your hostas look the way they do.

## The Flood

In 2019, the flood put four feet of water in our basement. It was the kind of morning where you stand at the top of the stairs and wonder whether seventy years can float away.

Then our neighbors showed up with shop vacs. They didn't call ahead, and nobody organized it. People we'd sold hinges and garden hoses to for years came through the door with their own equipment and started working. We cleaned up, dried out, and opened back up, and we did it because this town decided we were worth saving. We don't forget that.

## The People

Becker Hardware has 11 employees. They know the difference between a compression fitting and a flare fitting, and they'll walk you to the right aisle instead of pointing.

Doris has worked our register for 31 years. If you've shopped here, you've met Doris. Some of our customers come in as much to say hello as to buy anything, and we're fine with that.

## Why We're Different

We aren't trying to beat the big box stores on price. They're good at being big. We're good at being here. We'll help you figure out what's wrong before you buy the wrong part, and we'll sell you the one washer you need. When you're halfway through a project on a Saturday morning and something isn't working, the person who answers the phone has probably fixed the same thing in their own house.

That's what Walt started with $2,000, and it's what we're still doing.
