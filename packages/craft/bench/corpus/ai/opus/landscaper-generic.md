# Fall Lawn Care: Your Guide to a Healthier Lawn Next Spring

When the leaves start turning and the air gets crisp, it's tempting to put the mower away and forget about the yard until spring. Fall, though, is one of the most important seasons for your lawn. The work you do now shapes how your grass looks when warm weather returns.

Cooler air, warm soil, and steady moisture make autumn the ideal time for grass to recover from summer stress and build strong roots. Here's how to make the most of it.

## 1. Keep Mowing, but Adjust Your Height

Grass keeps growing in the fall, just more slowly. Keep mowing on a regular schedule until growth stops, usually after the first few hard frosts.

For the last two or three cuts of the season, lower your mower blade gradually to about 2 to 2.5 inches. Shorter grass is less likely to mat down under snow, which helps prevent snow mold and other fungal diseases. Don't scalp the lawn, though. Cutting too short stresses the grass right before winter.

## 2. Don't Let Leaves Pile Up

A blanket of fallen leaves might look pretty, but it blocks sunlight, traps moisture, and smothers the grass underneath. Left through winter, wet leaves can leave dead patches and invite disease.

You have a couple of options:

- **Rake or blow leaves regularly** instead of waiting until they've all fallen.
- **Mulch them with your mower.** A light layer of leaves can be shredded into small pieces and left on the lawn, where they break down and return nutrients to the soil. If you can still see grass through the shredded leaves, you're in good shape.

## 3. Aerate Compacted Soil

Over time, foot traffic, kids, pets, and mowing compact the soil. Compacted soil makes it hard for water, air, and nutrients to reach grass roots.

Core aeration pulls small plugs of soil out of the lawn, opening space for roots to breathe and grow. Fall is the best time to aerate cool-season lawns because the grass can recover quickly before winter dormancy. Leave the soil plugs on the surface. They'll break down naturally within a few weeks.

## 4. Overseed Thin or Bare Spots

If your lawn looks patchy after a hot, dry summer, fall is the perfect time to overseed. Warm soil helps seeds germinate, while cooler air temperatures reduce stress on young grass. There's also less competition from weeds this time of year.

For best results, overseed right after aerating. The holes give seed direct contact with the soil, which greatly improves germination. Keep the newly seeded areas consistently moist until the grass is established.

## 5. Feed Your Lawn Before Winter

If you fertilize only once a year, make it in the fall. A fall feeding helps grass store nutrients in its roots, which supports winter hardiness and a quicker, greener start in spring.

Look for a fertilizer formulated for fall application, and apply it while the grass is still actively growing. Follow the label directions carefully. More isn't better, and excess fertilizer can run off into storm drains and waterways.

## 6. Tackle Weeds Now

Broadleaf weeds like dandelions and clover are busy pulling nutrients down into their roots in the fall. That makes this an effective time to treat them, because weed control products get carried down to the roots too.

Spot-treat problem areas rather than blanketing the whole lawn. A thick, healthy lawn is also your best long-term defense against weeds, which is one more reason aerating and overseeding pay off.

## 7. Water Until the Ground Freezes

Fall often brings more rain, but don't assume your lawn is getting enough. Grass still needs about an inch of water per week, including rainfall, until the ground freezes. This is especially important for newly seeded areas.

Water deeply and less often in the early morning to encourage strong roots and reduce the risk of disease. Before the first hard freeze, be sure to shut off and drain your irrigation system and outdoor hoses to prevent damaged pipes.

## 8. Clean Up and Prepare Your Equipment

Once your final mow is done, give your equipment some attention before storing it:

- Clean grass and debris off the mower deck.
- Sharpen the blades so they're ready for spring.
- Drain the fuel or add a fuel stabilizer.
- Store tools in a dry place.

A little maintenance now saves time and repair costs later.

## Set Your Lawn Up for Success

Fall lawn care isn't complicated, but timing matters. Mowing, leaf cleanup, aeration, overseeding, fertilizing, and proper watering all work together to help your grass survive winter and come back thick and green in spring.
