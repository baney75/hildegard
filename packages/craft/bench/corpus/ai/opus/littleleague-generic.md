# Spring Newsletter

## Play Ball! The Season Is Almost Here

Families, players, coaches, and volunteers,

The gloves are coming out of the garage, the cleats are being tried on (and probably outgrown), and the fields are waking up after a long winter. Spring is here, and so is another season of Little League baseball and softball. We could not be more excited to see everyone back at the ballpark.

Whether this is your child's first season or their last before aging out, we're glad you're part of our league. Here's what you need to know as we head into opening day.

---

### Opening Day Celebration

Opening Day is our favorite tradition of the year, and this season will be no different. Teams will gather in uniform for the player parade, followed by the national anthem, the ceremonial first pitch, and the first games of the season.

The concession stand will be open all day, and we'll have music, photos, and plenty of chances to meet your child's teammates and their families. Bring a chair, bring the grandparents, and bring your loudest cheering voice.

**Please arrive early.** Parking fills up fast, and teams should check in with their managers at least 30 minutes before the parade begins.

---

### Team Assignments and First Practices

Team rosters have been finalized, and managers will be reaching out to families directly with practice schedules. If you haven't heard from your child's coach yet, please check your email (including the spam folder) before contacting the league.

A few reminders for the first practice:

- Bring a water bottle labeled with your child's name.
- Players should wear a glove, cleats, and athletic clothing. Baseball players need a protective cup; catchers and pitchers especially.
- Helmets are provided by the league, but players are welcome to bring their own if it meets current safety standards.
- Sunscreen is your friend, even on cloudy days.

---

### Picture Day

Team and individual photos will be taken early in the season. Order forms will be distributed by your team manager. Players should arrive in full uniform, hats on, ready to smile. These photos are some of the best keepsakes from the season, and the memory mates are always a hit with families.

---

### We Need You: Volunteer Opportunities

Our league runs entirely on volunteers. Every game you watch happens because a parent, grandparent, neighbor, or former player gave up a few hours of their time. This season, we're looking for help with:

- **Concession stand shifts** — Each family is asked to work at least one shift during the season. It's easier than it sounds, and it's a great way to meet other families.
- **Field preparation** — Lining fields, raking infields, and setting up before games. No experience needed; we'll show you how.
- **Umpires** — We are always in need of umpires. Training is provided, and teenagers looking for volunteer hours or a little extra experience are encouraged to sign up.
- **Team parents** — Help your manager with snack schedules, communication, and the end-of-season party.
- **Scorekeepers and pitch count recorders** — Every game needs one. If you're going to be in the stands anyway, why not help out?

If you'd like to help, talk to your team manager or stop by the concession stand and ask any board member. Every hour counts.

---

### Safety First

The safety of our players is our top priority. A few reminders for the season:

- **Pitch count rules** are enforced for every game. Managers are responsible for tracking pitch counts and required rest days, and scorekeepers will record them after each game.
- **Lightning policy:** If thunder is heard or lightning is seen, all players, coaches, and spectators must leave the field immediately. Play will not resume until 30 minutes after the last strike or rumble.
- **Concussion awareness:** Any player suspected of a head injury will be removed from play and must be cleared by a medical professional before returning.
- **Drop-off and pick-up:** Please don't leave younger players unattended at the fields. Coaches are there to coach, not babysit, and they'll appreciate a parent staying nearby.
- All managers and coaches have completed background checks and safety training as required by Little League International.

---

### A Word on Sportsmanship

Little League is about learning the game, building friendships, and having fun. It's not the World Series (not yet, anyway).

We ask every parent and spectator to cheer for great plays on both sides, to let the coaches coach, and to remember that the umpires are volunteers, often teenagers, doing their best. A kind word goes a long way. A hollered complaint from the bleachers does not.

Our players are watching how the adults behave. Let's show them what it looks like to win with humility and lose with grace.
