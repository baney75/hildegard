Admission is free.

**Preparing at home**
The most helpful thing you can do is encourage steady, consistent practice. Short, focused sessions most days work far better than long sessions right before the recital. In the final few weeks, it also helps to have your child play their piece for the family as a "practice performance." Playing for an audience, even a small one, builds real confidence.

**What to wear**
Recital attire is dressy but comfortable: something your child feels good in and can move easily in at the piano. Please choose shoes that allow them to use the pedal comfortably.

**Recital etiquette**
To give every performer the respect they deserve, I ask that families plan to stay for the full program, silence phones, and save entrances and exits for the applause between pieces. Photos and video are welcome as long as there's no flash.

**Reception**
If you'd like to contribute a snack or treat to share, I'd be grateful. A sign-up sheet will be available at lessons in the coming weeks.

Performing can feel a little nerve-wracking, and that's completely normal. I'll be working with each student to make sure they feel prepared and proud walking onto that stage. Every year I'm amazed by how much they grow through this experience.

Please reply to this email to let me know whether your child will be participating, and don't hesitate to reach out with any questions.

I can't wait to celebrate our students together!
