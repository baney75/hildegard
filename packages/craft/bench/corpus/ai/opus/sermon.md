# The Ones Who Go Looking

*Luke 15:1–10*

Luke tells us the tax collectors and sinners were gathering around Jesus, and the Pharisees didn't like it. "This man welcomes sinners and eats with them." So Jesus tells them a story. Then another. Then a third.

Today I want to stay with the first two: a shepherd with a hundred sheep who loses one, and a woman with ten silver coins who loses one.

Notice what these two stories have in common. Neither the sheep nor the coin does anything to get found. The sheep doesn't find its way back. The coin certainly doesn't. It's on the floor in the dark, under something, rolled into a corner. The whole weight of both stories falls on the one who goes looking.

The shepherd leaves the ninety-nine. The woman lights a lamp and sweeps the house. And she searches "carefully," Luke says, "until she finds it." That word *until* is doing a lot of work. It doesn't say how long. It doesn't promise it will be quick or tidy. It only says she didn't stop.

Some of you know this already, but I want to tell it again because it's been on my heart.

Last month one of the young men from our youth group lost his dad. We buried his father, and after the funeral he stopped coming. Not just to church. He stopped answering texts. He stepped away from all of us, and honestly, who could blame him? Grief does that. Sometimes the place that holds the most memories is the hardest place to walk into.

So on a Tuesday night, three of us drove over to his apartment. We didn't have a plan. We didn't bring a pamphlet or a speech. We knocked, and he came out, and we sat on his porch.

For an hour.

He never said a word.

I'll be honest with you. Driving home, I wasn't sure we'd done anything at all. I kept thinking of things I should have said. A verse I could have shared. Something wiser. We'd sat there in the cold and nobody had talked, and I wondered if we'd just made it more awkward for him.

Two weeks later he walked into youth group.

Now here's where I have to be careful, and I want you to be careful with me. It would be easy to turn this into a recipe. *Sit on a porch for an hour and they'll come back.* That isn't what happened, and it isn't what Jesus is teaching. I don't know what moved in that young man's heart over those two weeks. I don't know if our hour on the porch had anything to do with it. Maybe it did. Maybe it was something else entirely, something God was doing that none of us saw. And if he hadn't come back, we'd still have been right to go.

That's the thing about the shepherd and the woman with the lamp. Jesus doesn't tell us their technique. He doesn't say the shepherd used a particular call or the woman had a better broom. He just tells us they went looking, because what was lost mattered to them.

And then he tells us about the joy. The shepherd puts the sheep on his shoulders and calls his friends: *Rejoice with me.* The woman gathers her neighbors: *Rejoice with me.* "In the same way," Jesus says, "there is rejoicing in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner who repents."

Heaven throws a party over one.

Church, there are people in your life who have wandered off, or rolled into a dark corner, or just gone quiet. You may not know what to say to them. That's all right. You may say nothing at all. But you can still go. You can light a lamp. You can sit on the porch.

The finding belongs to God. The going belongs to us.

Amen.
