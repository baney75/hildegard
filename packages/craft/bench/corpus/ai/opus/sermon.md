**The Shepherd Who Sits on the Porch**
*Luke 15:1–10*

There's a detail in Luke 15 that's easy to miss. Jesus tells these stories because the religious leaders are grumbling. "This man welcomes sinners and eats with them." So he answers with a sheep and a coin.

Notice what Jesus doesn't do. He doesn't argue doctrine or hand them a five-step plan for church growth. He asks them a question: "Suppose one of you has a hundred sheep and loses one of them. Doesn't he leave the ninety-nine in the open country and go after the lost sheep until he finds it?"

*Until he finds it.* Not until it's convenient, and not until the sheep shows some sign of wanting to be found.

Then the coin. A woman lights a lamp, sweeps the house, and searches carefully. The coin can't do anything. It doesn't cry out or roll toward the light. It just lies there in the dust while someone who loves it gets down on her hands and knees.

I've been thinking about these stories because of something that happened in our church family last month.

One of the young men in our youth group lost his dad. We buried his father, we held him as best we could, and then he stopped coming. Nobody could blame him. Grief does strange things to a heart, and sometimes the place where you sang hymns at your father's funeral is the last place you can stand to be.

So on a Tuesday night, three of us drove over to his apartment. We didn't have a speech prepared. We knocked, and he came out, and we sat down on his porch.

For an hour, he didn't say a word.

Neither did we, really. A few of us tried at the start, and then it became clear that words weren't what he needed. So we sat. Cars went by. It got dark. Eventually we prayed quietly, told him we loved him, and drove home.

I'll be honest with you. On the drive back, I wasn't sure we'd done anything at all.

Two weeks later, he walked into youth group.

I want to be careful here, because I don't want you to hear this as a formula: sit on a porch for sixty minutes and the lost come home. That isn't how it works, and that isn't what Jesus is teaching. Some of you have sat on porches for years. Some of you have prayed for a son or a daughter or a spouse longer than that young man has been alive, and you're still waiting.

The point of Luke 15 isn't that seeking always works on our timeline. The point is *who God is.* He is the shepherd who goes out into the open country. He is the woman with the lamp and the broom. He doesn't wait for the lost to clean themselves up and find their way back. He comes looking.

And here's what struck me about that Tuesday night: the coin doesn't have to say anything to be found. The sheep doesn't have to explain itself. That young man didn't owe us a single word. He was loved before he spoke, and he would have been loved if he'd never come back.

Jesus ends both stories with a party. "Rejoice with me; I have found my lost sheep." Heaven throws a celebration over one person coming home. We had a little one of our own that night at youth group, and I suspect heaven was louder.

So who is on your heart? Who has drifted, gone quiet, or stopped answering? You don't have to have the right words. You may not get a response for a long time. But you can go. You can light the lamp. You can sit on the porch.

Because that's what our Shepherd did for us.

*— Pastor Dale Henning, Faith Bible Church, Lima, Ohio*
