# Winter Battery Care: How to Avoid Getting Stranded This Season

Every year, the first hard cold snap brings a wave of the same phone call: "My car won't start." Nine times out of ten, the culprit is the battery. The good news is that most winter battery failures are predictable, and preventable, with a little attention before the temperature drops.

## Why Cold Weather Is Hard on Batteries

A car battery produces power through a chemical reaction, and cold slows that reaction down. At 32°F, a battery delivers noticeably less of its rated power than it does at 80°F. At 0°F, the loss is far greater.

At the same time, cold makes your engine harder to turn over. Engine oil thickens, and the starter has to work harder. So you get a battery with less power available facing an engine that demands more. A battery that was barely hanging on in September often gives up in December.

## Warning Signs Your Battery Is Fading

Watch for these before you're stuck in a parking lot:

- **Slow, labored cranking.** If the engine sounds sluggish when you start it, don't ignore it.
- **Dim headlights or interior lights**, especially at startup.
- **The battery or check engine light** appearing on your dash.
- **Clicking sounds** when you turn the key or press the start button.
- **Corrosion** on the terminals, which looks like white, blue, or green crusty buildup.
- **Age.** Most car batteries last three to five years. If yours is past the three-year mark, it deserves a test.

## What You Can Do Before Winter Hits

**1. Get it tested.** A battery can seem fine and still be close to failing. A professional load test measures how well it performs under real demand, not just its surface voltage. At our shop, this is quick, and it can save you from a tow bill.

**2. Keep the terminals clean.** Corrosion restricts the flow of electricity. Cleaning the terminals and making sure the cable connections are tight helps your battery deliver all the power it has.

**3. Check the charging system.** Sometimes the battery isn't the problem at all. A weak alternator won't keep it charged, and a new battery will fail the same way. Testing the whole system tells you the real story.

**4. Take longer drives.** Short trips don't give the alternator enough time to replenish what the starter used. If most of your driving is a few minutes at a time, take a longer drive once a week to keep the battery topped up.

**5. Turn off accessories before shutting down.** Heated seats, the defroster, lights, and the radio all draw power. Switching them off before you turn the engine off, and before you start it, takes load off the battery.

**6. Park smart.** If you can, park in a garage. Even an unheated garage stays warmer than the outdoors. In extreme cold, a battery blanket or block heater can make a real difference.

**7. Secure the battery.** A battery that vibrates in its tray can suffer internal damage. Make sure the hold-down is tight.

## If Your Car Won't Start

First, don't keep cranking. Repeated attempts can drain what's left and strain the starter. Try a few seconds at a time with a pause in between. If you have jumper cables or a portable jump starter, follow the instructions in your owner's manual and connect them in the correct order. If you're unsure, call for help rather than risk damaging your car's electronics.

Once you get going, don't assume you're in the clear. A jump gets you moving, but it doesn't fix whatever drained the battery. Have it tested soon.
