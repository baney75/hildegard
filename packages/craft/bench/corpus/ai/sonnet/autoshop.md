## Why Cold Weather Is So Hard on Batteries

A battery is a chemical device, and chemistry slows down when it gets cold. At 32 degrees, a battery loses about a third of its starting power. At zero, it loses about half.

Meanwhile, your engine needs more power to start in the cold, because the oil is thicker and everything is harder to turn. So just when your car demands the most from the battery, the battery has the least to give.

A battery that works fine in October can fail on the first truly cold morning. That's usually not sudden. The battery has been weakening for months, and the cold just exposes it.

## How Long Should a Battery Last?

Most batteries last 3 to 5 years. Here in Wyoming, the ones I see average about 4. If yours is approaching that age, or past it, don't wait for it to tell you it's done. It will, and it'll pick the worst possible moment.

Not sure how old your battery is? Many have a date code stamped or stickered on the case. If you can't find it, stop in and I'll look.

## Warning Signs

Two things to watch for:

- **Slow cranking.** If the engine turns over sluggishly, like it's struggling to wake up, the battery is losing strength.
- **Dimming dash lights at idle.** If your dashboard lights go dim while the engine is idling, the electrical system is under strain.

If you notice either one, get the battery tested before the first cold snap. Don't wait to see if it gets better. It won't.

## Clean Your Terminals

Corrosion on the battery terminals, that crusty white or bluish-green buildup, can keep a good battery from delivering its power. You can fix this yourself with things you probably already have:

1. Turn the car off and disconnect the battery cables, negative first.
2. Mix baking soda with a little water to make a paste.
3. Scrub the terminals and cable clamps with a wire brush and the paste until they're clean and shiny.
4. Wipe everything dry, reconnect the cables (positive first this time), and make sure they're snug.

Wear gloves and eye protection. Battery acid is nothing to mess around with.
