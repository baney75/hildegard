# Don't Let Wyoming's Cold Leave You Stranded: A Winter Battery Care Guide

By Luis Moreno, Moreno Auto, Cheyenne, Wyoming

Every January, my phone starts ringing before sunrise. The story is almost always the same: the truck was fine yesterday, and this morning it just clicks. In the first week of last January alone, I jump-started 42 cars. Nearly every one of those calls could have been avoided with ten minutes of attention before the cold arrived.

Here's what every Cheyenne driver should know about winter batteries, and what to do about it now.

## Why Cold Is So Hard on Your Battery

A battery is a chemical device, and cold slows chemistry down. At 32 degrees, a battery loses about a third of its starting power. At zero, it loses about half. Meanwhile, cold engine oil gets thick and your engine needs more power to turn over, not less.

So on the coldest mornings, your battery is at its weakest just when your engine asks the most of it. A battery that handled September without complaint can fail on the first hard freeze.

## How Long Batteries Last Here

Most batteries last 3 to 5 years. In Wyoming, the ones I see average about 4. If yours is approaching that mark, or already past it, treat winter as a deadline rather than a suggestion.

## Warning Signs

Watch for these two in particular:

- **Slow cranking.** If the engine turns over sluggishly, as if it's working to get going, the battery is struggling.
- **Dimming dash lights at idle.** If your dashboard lights fade while the engine is idling, the electrical system is not getting what it needs.

If you notice either one, get the battery tested before the first cold snap. Waiting until the temperature drops is how people end up on my jump-start list.

## A Simple Fix You Can Do Yourself

Sometimes the problem isn't the battery at all. Corroded terminals, that crusty white or bluish buildup around the posts, can keep power from flowing properly. You can clean them at home:

1. Turn the engine off and disconnect the battery cables, negative first.
2. Mix baking soda with a little water to make a paste.
3. Scrub the terminals and cable clamps with a wire brush.
4. Wipe everything clean and dry, then reconnect, positive first.

It costs almost nothing and can make a real difference. If the terminals are badly eaten away, or you aren't comfortable working around a battery, stop by and we'll take care of it.
