# What to Bring on a Fall Steelhead Float Trip

Fall steelhead fishing is some of the best days you'll spend on the water. The fish are fresh from the salt, the colors on the banks are turning, and the mornings have that crisp edge that makes the first hot coffee taste better than it has any right to. It's also the season when a poorly packed bag can turn a great day into a long, cold one.

Here's what I tell every angler before they step into the boat.

## Dress in Layers, and Skip the Cotton

Fall weather on the river swings hard. You might launch in a 35-degree fog and be shedding layers by early afternoon. Build your system so you can adjust.

- **Base layer:** Merino wool or synthetic, top and bottom. Cotton holds moisture and will chill you fast.
- **Mid layer:** A fleece or lightweight puffy that you can pull on and off.
- **Outer shell:** A waterproof, breathable rain jacket. Rain is always a possibility, and wind on the water cuts straight through anything less.
- **Socks:** Bring two pairs of wool socks. If you're wading, a dry pair for the ride home is a small luxury you'll appreciate.

## Footwear and Waders

If we'll be getting out of the boat to fish a run, you'll want waders and wading boots. Breathable waders with room for layers underneath are ideal. Make sure your boots fit comfortably over thick socks, and check for leaks before the trip, not at the boat ramp.

If you'd rather stay in the boat, waterproof boots with good traction are still a must. Wet decks are slick.

## Hands, Head, and Neck

Cold hands end fishing days early. Bring:

- Fingerless or fold-back fishing gloves, plus a warmer pair for the run between spots
- A wool or fleece beanie
- A buff or neck gaiter
- A brimmed hat and polarized sunglasses. Polarized lenses help you read the water and protect your eyes from stray hooks and weights

## Rain Gear and a Dry Bag

Even a clear forecast can change by lunch. Pack a dry bag for your extra layer, phone, keys, and anything else that shouldn't get wet. A small one is plenty.

## Food, Water, and Something Hot

I'll have lunch and drinks covered on guided trips unless we've arranged otherwise, but I always encourage you to bring a few things of your own:

- A water bottle you can refill
- A thermos of coffee, tea, or cocoa
- Snacks you like. Steelhead fishing involves a lot of patient casting, and energy bars or jerky go a long way

## Fishing Gear: What I Provide and What You Can Bring

On my trips, rods, reels, terminal tackle, and bait or flies are provided. If you have a favorite rod or setup, you're welcome to bring it. Just let me know ahead of time so we can make sure it's matched to the water we'll be fishing.

A few personal items worth bringing:

- Your valid fishing license and any required steelhead or salmon endorsement or punch card
- A pair of pliers or forceps if you have a preferred set
- Polarized sunglasses (yes, again, they matter that much)

## Comfort and Safety Items

- **Sunscreen and lip balm.** Fall sun reflecting off the water still burns.
- **Personal medications.** Anything you need during the day should come with you.
- **Camera or phone.** Steelhead are beautiful fish, and you'll want the photo. A waterproof case or lanyard is a smart add-on.
- **Cash for a tip.** If your guide earned it, this is the traditional way to say thanks.

You'll wear a life jacket in the boat. I have properly fitted ones available, but you're welcome to bring your own if you prefer.

## What to Leave at Home

- Heavy cotton hoodies and jeans
- Anything you'd be heartbroken to lose overboard
- Too much gear. A boat has limited space, and a simple, well-chosen kit beats a pile of "just in case" items

## A Quick Checklist

- Base layers (wool or synthetic)
- Fleece or puffy mid layer
- Waterproof rain jacket
- Waders and wading boots (or waterproof boots)
- Extra wool socks
- Gloves and a warm hat
- Buff or neck gaiter
- Brimmed hat and polarized sunglasses
- Dry bag
- Water bottle and thermos
- Snacks
- Fishing license and endorsements
- Sunscreen and lip balm
- Camera or phone
- Tip
