# What to Bring on a Fall Steelhead Float Trip

Fall steelhead fishing is some of the best time you can spend on the water. The crowds thin out, the leaves turn, and fresh chrome fish push up the river with the rain. But a day in a drift boat in October or November can swing from warm sun to sideways sleet, and a little preparation is the difference between a great day and a long one. Here's what I tell every angler to pack before they step in my boat.

## Dress in Layers, and Skip the Cotton

The river is always colder than the parking lot. Start with a moisture-wicking base layer in merino or synthetic fabric. Add a fleece or insulated mid-layer, then top it with a quality waterproof rain jacket. Cotton holds water and steals your warmth, so leave the jeans and hoodies at home.

Bring a warm hat and a buff or neck gaiter. You'll lose heat fast sitting still in the boat between drifts, and you'll be glad to have them when the wind picks up.

## Footwear That Works

Waterproof boots or chest waders with wading boots are the best choice. If we're stopping to fish a run on foot, you'll want felt or studded soles for traction on slick river rock. Pack an extra pair of wool socks. Dry socks at lunch feel like a luxury.

## Gloves and Hand Warmers

Cold hands make it hard to tie knots and fight fish. Bring fingerless fishing gloves or a thin pair of neoprene gloves, plus a heavier pair for the ride between spots. Chemical hand warmers are cheap, light, and worth tossing in every pocket.

## Polarized Sunglasses

Polarized glasses cut glare so you can read the water, spot seams and holding lies, and keep an eye on your float. They also protect your eyes from stray hooks. Bring a pair with a retainer strap, because things that fall in the river stay in the river.

## Food, Water, and Something Hot

We'll be out most of the day, so pack a lunch and snacks. I keep a cooler in the boat, but bring what you like to eat. Drink more water than you think you need, since cold weather hides dehydration. A thermos of coffee, tea, or hot cocoa is one of the best things you can bring. Nothing beats a hot drink halfway through a drift.

## Gear: What I Provide and What You Can Bring

I supply rods, reels, terminal tackle, flies, and everything you need to hook a fish. If you have a favorite steelhead setup, you're welcome to bring it. Just let me know ahead of time so we can make sure it suits the water we'll be fishing.

What you'll want to bring yourself:

- Valid fishing license and steelhead endorsement or punch card, as required in your state
- Sunscreen and lip balm (fall sun on the water is still strong)
- A small dry bag for your phone, keys, and wallet
- Any personal medications
- A camera or phone for photos of your fish

## A Few Extras Worth Packing

- **A change of clothes** left in the vehicle for the ride home
- **A towel** for drying hands between fish
- **Rain pants** if the forecast looks wet
- **A personal flotation device.** I have life jackets on board, but you're welcome to bring your own
- **Patience and a good attitude.** Steelhead are the fish of a thousand casts, and that's part of the draw

## Final Thoughts

Fall steelhead fishing rewards the prepared. Dress warm, bring food and a hot drink, and leave the rest to me. When that first bright fish takes the fly and the reel starts screaming, you'll forget all about the cold.
