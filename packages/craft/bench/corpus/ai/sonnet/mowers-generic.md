# Putting Your Lawn Mower Away for Winter: A Simple Checklist

The last leaves are coming down, the grass has stopped growing, and your mower has earned its rest. What you do in the next hour or two will decide whether it starts on the first pull next spring or ends up on our workbench with a gummed-up carburetor.

Here's how to put your mower away properly.

## 1. Deal With the Fuel

Fuel is the biggest cause of spring starting trouble. Gasoline with ethanol starts breaking down in as little as 30 days. It leaves gummy deposits that clog carburetor jets and fuel lines.

You have two good options:

- **Add fuel stabilizer.** Fill the tank with fresh gas, add stabilizer according to the label, and run the engine for five to ten minutes so the treated fuel reaches the carburetor.
- **Run it dry.** Shut off the fuel valve if your mower has one and let the engine run until it stops. This works, but some fuel can still sit in the carburetor bowl, so stabilizer is the safer choice.

Whichever you choose, don't leave old gas from last summer sitting in the tank all winter.

## 2. Change the Oil

Used oil holds moisture and acids that can damage the engine while it sits. Run the engine for a few minutes to warm the oil, then drain it while it flows easily. Refill with the grade listed in your owner's manual. Starting spring with clean oil is one of the best things you can do for engine life.

## 3. Disconnect the Spark Plug

Before you tip the mower or touch the blade, pull the spark plug wire. It's a simple safety step that prevents accidental starts. While you're there, remove the plug and look at it. If it's black, oily, or worn, replace it. A new plug is inexpensive and makes for easier starting.

## 4. Clean the Deck

Wet, packed-on grass holds moisture against the metal and causes rust. With the spark plug wire disconnected, tip the mower with the air filter and carburetor side facing up. This keeps oil and fuel from running where they shouldn't. Scrape the underside of the deck and rinse or brush it clean. Let it dry completely.

## 5. Inspect and Sharpen the Blade

Winter is a good time to deal with the blade. Look for bends, cracks, and deep nicks. A dull blade tears grass instead of cutting it, which stresses your lawn and your engine. If it needs sharpening or replacing, bring it to us now, before the spring rush.

## 6. Check the Air Filter

A paper filter that's dirty or oil-soaked should be replaced. A foam filter can be washed with mild soap and water, dried fully, and lightly re-oiled. Starting the season with a clean filter helps the engine breathe and run well.

## 7. Take Care of the Battery

If you have an electric-start mower or a riding mower, remove the battery if you can. Clean the terminals and store it in a cool, dry place, ideally on a maintainer or trickle charger. A battery left in an unheated shed can lose its charge and may not recover from a hard freeze.

## 8. Pick a Good Storage Spot

Store the mower somewhere dry and sheltered, like a garage or a shed. Avoid leaving it outside under a tarp, which traps moisture and invites rust and nesting mice. If you do cover it, use a breathable cover and keep it off bare dirt. Consider setting it on boards or a mat to keep the wheels dry.

If you have a riding mower, also check tire pressure, grease the fittings listed in your manual, and consider putting it on blocks to keep weight off the tires.
