# How to Put Your Lawn Mower Away for Winter (So It Starts in Spring)

Every spring, our shop fills up with mowers that won't start. Nine times out of ten, the problem began months earlier, when the mower was pushed into a corner of the garage in November with a half tank of gas and a season's worth of grass caked underneath. Twenty minutes of prep now can save you a repair bill and a lost Saturday next April.

Here's how to do it right.

## 1. Deal with the Fuel First

Fuel is the number one cause of spring no-starts. Gasoline starts to break down in as little as 30 days. Ethanol-blended fuel also attracts moisture, and the result is varnish and gum that clog your carburetor's tiny passages.

You have two good options:

**Option A: Stabilize and store.** Fill the tank most of the way with fresh gas, add fuel stabilizer according to the label, then run the engine for five to ten minutes so the treated fuel reaches the carburetor. A fairly full tank leaves less air space for moisture to condense.

**Option B: Run it dry.** Shut off the fuel valve if your mower has one and let the engine run until it stalls. This works, but it isn't foolproof, since some fuel residue can remain in the carburetor bowl. If you go this route, we still recommend stabilizer in the last bit of fuel.

Whichever you pick, do not leave old, untreated gas sitting in the tank all winter. If you have gas left over from the summer, don't pour it into a mower you plan to store. Use fresh fuel.

## 2. Disconnect the Spark Plug

Before you do any work under or around the mower, pull the spark plug wire off the plug. This prevents the engine from accidentally turning over while your hands are near the blade. For battery-start models, disconnect the battery as well.

## 3. Change the Oil

Used oil holds acids and contaminants that can slowly damage internal parts during a long idle period. Run the engine for a few minutes to warm the oil so it drains easily, then change it while the engine is still warm (not hot). Check your owner's manual for the correct type and amount. Dispose of the old oil at a recycling center or auto parts store that accepts used oil.

## 4. Clean the Deck and Check the Blade

Tip the mower with the carburetor and air filter facing up, never the other way, so oil and gas don't run into places they shouldn't. Scrape off caked grass and debris from the underside of the deck. Wet, packed clippings hold moisture and cause rust.

While you're down there, inspect the blade. If it's dull, bent, or nicked, now is the perfect time to sharpen or replace it. Shops are generally less busy in winter, so you'll often get faster turnaround than in the spring rush. If you aren't comfortable removing and balancing a blade yourself, bring it to us.

## 5. Replace the Air Filter and Spark Plug

These are cheap parts that make a big difference. A clogged air filter chokes the engine, and a worn spark plug makes starting harder. Replace both now, and spring startup will be easier. Foam filters can often be washed in soapy water, dried completely, and lightly re-oiled. Paper filters should simply be replaced when dirty.

## 6. Clean and Lubricate

Wipe down the whole mower, including the engine's cooling fins, which can clog with grass and dust. Check wheels, cables, and the height-adjustment levers, and apply a light lubricant where metal moves against metal. A quick spray of protectant on exposed metal surfaces helps discourage rust.

## 7. Charge the Battery (If You Have One)

Electric and battery-start mowers need attention too. Remove the battery and store it indoors, away from freezing temperatures. Lithium-ion batteries generally keep best at around a 40 to 60 percent charge. Check it once or twice over the winter. For lead-acid batteries, use a maintenance charger to keep them topped off.

## 8. Choose the Right Storage Spot

Store your mower in a dry, sheltered place, such as a garage or shed. Avoid spots with big temperature swings or moisture. If you have to store it outside, use a breathable cover. A plastic tarp that traps condensation can do more harm than good. Keep the mower off bare ground, ideally on a pallet or a level concrete floor.

If you can, fold down the handle and store it in a way that frees up room, but make sure no cables are pinched or kinked.

## Your Winter Storage Checklist

- Stabilize the fuel and run the engine, or run the tank dry
- Disconnect the spark plug wire
- Change the oil
- Clean the deck and inspect or sharpen the blade
- Replace the air filter and spark plug
- Clean and lubricate moving parts
- Remove and store the battery properly
- Store in a dry, protected location
