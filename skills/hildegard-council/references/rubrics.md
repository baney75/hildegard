# The council's rubrics

Six critics. Each one reads only its own section, the brief and the render. `scripts/council.py brief` cuts the
right section out of this file for a role and a medium, so edit the rubric here and nowhere else.

How every critic scores, whatever the role:

- Score each criterion 1 to 5 against the anchors. 3 means "a client would accept it and forget it". 5 is rare: a
  working professional would put it in a portfolio. Never give a 5 you can't defend by pointing at the render.
- Every score below 4 comes with a fix. A fix names the exact element and the exact change: a size in px, a color in
  hex, a weight, a word to cut, a shape to remove. "Improve the hierarchy" is not a fix; "make the date 72 px, twice
  the time line, and move it above the address" is.
- A hard fail is a fault that makes the work unusable as it stands. Only the kinds listed for your role count. Name
  where it is.
- Tag every fix with one area: `concept`, `mark`, `wordmark`, `lockup`, `type`, `color`, `contrast`, `small-size`,
  `layout`, `copy`, `imagery`, `states`, `platform`, `production`, `motion`, `sound`. The tally counts agreement by
  area, so pick the closest one.

## Art director

You decide whether the work has one idea and says it with authority. You look at the whole first, then the parts.

| Criterion | 1 | 3 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One idea | you can't say what it is about | an idea is there but shares the stage with a second one | you can say the idea in six words, and every part serves it |
| Hierarchy | everything is the same size and weight | a first thing to see, then a muddle | first, second, third are unmistakable at a glance and at arm's length |
| From the subject | stock imagery or a category cliche (swoosh, globe, lightbulb, generic leaf, gear, house roof, map pin, check mark, abstract people) | a relevant symbol, but one a competitor could use unchanged | something only this client has: their place, their tool, their story, their letters |
| Craft and consistency | misaligned edges, mixed corner styles, stroke weights that wander | clean, but some parts drawn in a different hand | one hand throughout: one stroke weight logic, one corner radius, optical balance |

Hard fails you may call: `cliche` (the main mark is a category cliche), `two-ideas` (two concepts fight and neither
wins), `off-brief` (it is for a different client, audience or purpose than the brief).

### logo

Look at the mark alone at 512 and at 32 px. A strong mark is a silhouette you could draw from memory after one look.

### brand

The guide is one idea carried through logo, color, type, voice and imagery. Check that each section belongs to the
same client.

### screen

The screen has one job. Find the one primary action; if there are two of equal weight, that is the fix.

### page, image, motion

The first frame or first screen states the idea. For motion, judge the arc: a start, a turn, an end with the ask.

## Typographer

You care about letters: the choice of typeface, how they sit together, and how they read at the size they are used.

| Criterion | 1 | 3 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Typeface fit | a default (Arial, Inter on slate, Papyrus) or a face at odds with the client | appropriate but generic for the category | a face whose shapes say something true about the client |
| Spacing and kerning | letters touch, collide, or show holes (look at pairs like Ty, AV, LT, r., "ff") | even, but the default metrics with one or two loose pairs | every gap looks equal when you turn the render upside down |
| Scale and contrast | sizes too close to rank (16, 17, 18) | a scale, but timid | a clear scale (about 1.25 to 1.5 between steps), weights doing work |
| Weight at size | thin or light weights at small sizes, hairline serifs that vanish | readable but strained at the smallest size | every size uses a weight that holds up there |

Hard fails you may call: `misspelled` (a word differs from the brief), `colliding` (letters or lines overlap or are
cut off), `live-text` (a logo meant for print still uses live text: the checker's `text` check fails).

### logo

Read the wordmark letter by letter against the brief's spelling. Then flip the render upside down in your head and
look at the spaces between letters: the widest and the tightest gap are your fixes, with a value in font units or px.

### brand

The pairing: one display face, one text face, at most. Name the scale steps you see; a missing step or a step too
close to its neighbor is a fix.

### screen

Body text at least 16 px on the web, 17 pt on iOS, 16 sp on Android; captions no smaller than 11 to 12. Line length
under about 70 characters. Numbers in tables use tabular figures.

### page, image, motion

Titles in a real typeface, never generated by an image model. On-screen text in motion stays up long enough to read
twice.

## Brand strategist

You think about the people it is for and the people it competes with.

| Criterion | 1 | 3 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fits the audience | the audience in the brief wouldn't recognize themselves in it | right in general, wrong in a detail (too corporate for a farm stand, too cute for a law firm) | the audience would say "that's us" or "that's for me" |
| Stands apart | it looks like the category leader or every competitor (name what it looks like) | some difference, mostly in color | you could tell it from its three nearest competitors in black and white |
| True to the brief | claims or tone the brief doesn't support | true but bland | says one true, specific thing about this client |
| Holds as a system | works in one place only | works in two or three places the brief names | you can see it on every place the brief names (sign, truck, app icon, cup, polo) |

Hard fails you may call: `invented-copy` (a tagline, claim, number, year, award or fact not in the brief),
`lookalike` (close enough to a known brand to confuse or invite a dispute), `wrong-audience`.

### logo

Picture it on each place the brief names. Name the nearest competitor or category look it resembles.

### brand

The voice lines and the do and don't examples must sound like this client, not like any company.

### screen

The words on the screen are the client's own (their product names, their prices). Placeholder or generic marketing
copy is a fix.

### page, image, motion

The piece could not be moved to a competitor by swapping the name.

## Legibility and access

You check that people can read and use it: at a glance, at small sizes, with low vision or color blindness.

| Criterion | 1 | 3 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Thumbnail | shrink it to an eighth: you can't read the name or the main line | the main line reads, the rest is mush | the name or headline and the one action read at an eighth |
| Contrast | essential text under 3:1 | body text 4.5:1, some secondary text under | every text at least 4.5:1 (3:1 for large text), measured, not guessed |
| Small sizes | breaks at the smallest stated size (16 px favicon, 1 inch print) | survives, with detail lost | the same shape at 16 px as at 512 |
| Color independence | meaning carried by red versus green or by color alone | mostly fine | works in grayscale and for red-green color blindness |

Hard fails you may call: `illegible` (essential text or the name can't be read at its intended size),
`broken-small` (the mark falls apart at 16 or 32 px, or hairlines under 1 px at 32 px), `low-contrast` (essential text
under 3:1), `tiny-targets` (a tap target under 24 px).

### logo

Use the sheet's 16 and 32 px views, shown magnified with hard pixels. Compare their silhouette with the 512 px mark.

### brand

Check the contrast table's numbers against the pairs the guide recommends for text. A pair recommended for body text
under 4.5:1 is a hard fail.

### screen

Tap targets at least 44 by 44 pt on iOS, 48 by 48 dp on Android, 24 by 24 px with a pointer. Focus states visible.
Nothing important only in color.

### page, image, motion

Captions and titles: size, contrast and time on screen.

## The customer

You are the person in the brief who would meet this in real life, on the street, on a phone, on a shelf. Write as that
person: "I'm a parent at the pool gate, I have ten seconds".

| Criterion | 1 | 3 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Would I stop | I'd scroll or walk past | I'd notice it if I were already looking | it would catch me when I wasn't looking |
| Do I understand | I can't tell what this business or screen is for | I get it after reading the small print | I get it in three seconds |
| Do I know what to do | no next step, or two that compete | a next step, if I hunt for it | the one thing to do is obvious (call, book, tap, remember the name) |
| Do I trust it | it looks like a scam, a template or a stock ad | it looks fine | it looks like a real, careful business I'd hand money to |

Hard fails you may call: `confusing` (you can't tell what it is or what to do), `untrustworthy` (it looks like a
template, a scam or a different kind of business).

### logo

Would you remember it tomorrow? Describe it as you would to a friend in one sentence; if you can't, that is a fix.

### brand

Would the guide's voice examples make you want to read more from this business?

### screen

Act out the task the brief names, step by step, on this screen. Where do you hesitate?

### page, image, motion

The moment you'd stop scrolling, or the moment you'd leave.

## Production

You are the person who has to make it real: the sign shop, the embroiderer, the printer, the developer.

| Criterion | 1 | 3 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One color and reversed | needs color or a gradient to read | readable in one color with some loss | identical in one color, reversed, and on a photo |
| Scales | fine detail that fills in or drops out when small | holds at the minimum size the sheet states, barely | strokes and gaps thick enough to embroider at 1 inch and engrave |
| Files and system | missing files, live text, unnamed colors | most files, some guessing needed | every lockup, size and color named and ready to hand to a vendor |
| Built to last | built from one-off values | mostly tokens or a grid | everything on a grid or from tokens; changing one value changes all |

Hard fails you may call: `needs-gradient` (it doesn't read without a gradient, shadow or transparency),
`hairlines` (the checker reports lines under 1 px at 32 px), `unlicensed-font` (a font not under a license that allows
the use, such as a font not on Google Fonts with no license named), `missing-deliverable`.

### logo

Read the sheet's measured lines: colors, minimum sizes, one-color edge loss, thin features. Imagine it stitched on a
polo, cut in vinyl for a truck door, and stamped in one ink on kraft paper.

### brand

Hex values, contrast numbers and font names are present and consistent with what you see. Files are listed.

### screen

Every state the brief implies is designed (empty, loading, error, offline, full). Built from tokens. Platform
conventions are respected: safe areas, the platform's navigation pattern, system type sizes.

### page, image, motion

Exports at the sizes and formats the brief names; loudness, codecs and frame size where it applies.
